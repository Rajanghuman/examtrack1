SUBJECT 1: ENGLISH COMPREHENSION

PART A: GRAMMAR FOUNDATIONS

Chapter 1: Nouns & Case Rules

Textbook Definition: A noun is the name of a person, place, thing, quality, state, or action. Case represents the grammatical relationship of a noun or pronoun to other words in a sentence, including Subjective, Objective, Possessive, and Vocative cases.

Simple Explanation: A noun is a naming tag for everything in our world. Case tells us how that name interacts with other words—especially who owns what (Possessive Case) using the apostrophe ($'s$).

Examples:

Incorrect: This is Ravi's and Amit's joint business partner.

Correct: This is Ravi and Amit's joint business partner. (When two nouns share joint possession of a single thing/person, the apostrophe goes only on the last noun).

Incorrect: These are Ravi's and Amit's respective cars.

Correct: These are Ravi's and Amit's respective cars. (When possession is separate, both nouns get an apostrophe).

Incorrect: The table's wood is of premium quality.

Correct: The wood of the table is of premium quality. (Do not use apostrophe $+ s$ for non-living objects).

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (Living vs. Non-living): Use $'s$ with living entities (e.g., the boy's book, the lion's mane) and personified objects (e.g., nature's fury, at duty's call). For non-living things, use the "Of-phrase" structure.

Rule 2 (The Hissing Sound): For plural nouns ending in "s", or words producing a strong hissing sound, add only an apostrophe at the end ($'$), without an extra "$s$".

Correct: Girls' hostel, Keats' poetry, for conscience' sake.

Rule 3 (Compound Nouns): In compound nouns, the apostrophe is attached to the last word.

Correct: My sister-in-law's house (not sister's-in-law house).

Desi Mnemonic: "P-C-C-M-A" $\rightarrow$ Pappu Can't Clear Many Abstract-exams.

P $\rightarrow$ Proper (Unique name: Delhi, Rahul)

C $\rightarrow$ Common (General class: city, boy)

C $\rightarrow$ Collective (Group: jury, crew, flock)

M $\rightarrow$ Material (Raw substance: iron, milk, gold)

A $\rightarrow$ Abstract (State/Emotion: honesty, grief, anger)

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Pluralizing uncountable/abstract nouns (sceneries, furnitures, informations, baggages, luggages, mischiefs, poetries, advices).

Safeguard: These nouns are strictly singular and uncountable. To express quantity, use helper phrases like "a piece of advice" or "items of furniture". Never use $a/an$ directly before them.

Chapter 2: Pronouns (Types & Antecedent Traps)

Textbook Definition: A pronoun is a word used instead of a noun to prevent repetitive phrasing. An antecedent is the specific noun or noun phrase that the pronoun refers to, with which the pronoun must agree in number, gender, and person.

Simple Explanation: Pronouns are substitute words like he, she, they, it or who. The antecedent is the original name we are replacing. If the original name is singular, the pronoun must be singular!

Examples:

Incorrect: Each of the female doctors submitted their medical report.

Correct: Each of the female doctors submitted her medical report. (The antecedent "Each" is singular and feminine here).

Incorrect: It is me who helped you.

Correct: It is I who helped you. (Use subjective case pronoun after "It is" or "It was").

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (Pronoun Order): For polite, positive, or general statements, order pronouns as 2nd Person $\rightarrow$ 3rd Person $\rightarrow$ 1st Person ($231$).

Example: You, he and I are going to study.

For confessions, mistakes, or negative situations, use 1st Person $\rightarrow$ 2nd Person $\rightarrow$ 3rd Person ($123$).

Example: I, you and he made the mistake.

Rule 2 (Relative Pronoun "That"): Use "that" instead of "who" or "which" after superlative adjectives, and words like same, only, all, any, none, nothing, little.

Example: This is the best book that I have ever read.

Desi Mnemonic: "PRIDE" $\rightarrow$ Personal (I, you, they), Relative (who, which, that), Indefinite (someone, none), Demonstrative (this, those), Emphatic (myself, himself).

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Using "whom" when the sentence requires a subject, or "who" when it requires an object.

Safeguard: Put "He" or "Him" in place of the relative pronoun. If "He" (subject) fits, use who. If "Him" (object) fits, use whom.

Example: The boy who/whom I met yesterday was smart. (I met him $\rightarrow$ whom).

Chapter 3: Subject-Verb Agreement (High-Yield SSC Rules)

Textbook Definition: A verb must agree with its subject in number and person. A singular subject requires a singular verb, and a plural subject requires a plural verb.

Simple Explanation: Match the action word to the owner. If one person does the action, use a singular verb (usually ends in "s" like runs, is, has). If multiple people do the action, use a plural verb (run, are, have).

Examples:

Incorrect: The prime minister along with his cabinet ministers have arrived.

Correct: The prime minister along with his cabinet ministers has arrived. (When joined by "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject: "The prime minister").

Incorrect: Neither of the two answers were correct.

Correct: Neither of the two answers was correct. ("Neither" of two objects is singular).

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (Connecting Phrases): When two subjects are connected by as well as, along with, together with, with, accompanied by, in addition to, like, unlike, the verb agrees strictly with the first subject.

Rule 2 (Alternative Conjunctions): When connected by either... or, neither... nor, not only... but also, the verb agrees with the closest subject.

Example: Neither the teacher nor the students are present.

Rule 3 (Collective Nouns): Collective nouns (jury, family, committee) take a singular verb when acting as one unit, but a plural verb when divided in opinion or action.

Example: The jury was unanimous. vs. The jury were divided in their opinions.

Desi Mnemonic: "SING-SING, PLUR-PLUR" $\rightarrow$ Singular subject $\rightarrow$ Singular verb. Plural subject $\rightarrow$ Plural verb. Remember, adding "s" makes a noun plural (boys), but it makes a verb singular (runs).

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Treating "Many a" or "Each/Every" as plural because they represent large groups.

Safeguard: "Many a" and "Each of the..." are strictly singular.

Wrong: Many a students are absent.

Right: Many a student is absent.

Chapter 4: Verbs, Tenses & Conditional Sentences

Textbook Definition: A verb represents an action, occurrence, or state of being. Tense indicates the time frame of an action (Past, Present, Future). Conditional sentences contain a main clause and a conditional dependent clause (often starting with if, unless, when, as soon as).

Simple Explanation: Tenses tell us when something happened. Conditional sentences are "if-then" rule sheets. There are set matching combinations for tenses in conditional clauses that you must memorize.

Examples:

Incorrect: If I will win the lottery, I will buy a car.

Correct: If I win the lottery, I will buy a car. (Do not use future tense in the "if-clause").

Incorrect: I have seen him yesterday.

Correct: I saw him yesterday. (Never use Present Perfect with past time markers like yesterday, last year, in 1947).

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (The Three Conditional Equations):

Type 1 (Real): $\text{If} + \text{Simple Present } (V_1/V_5) \rightarrow \text{Simple Future } (\text{will} + V_1)$

Type 2 (Imaginary): $\text{If} + \text{Simple Past } (V_2 / \text{were}) \rightarrow \text{Conditional } (\text{would} + V_1)$

Type 3 (Unfulfilled Past): $\text{If} + \text{Past Perfect } (\text{had} + V_3) \rightarrow \text{Perfect Conditional } (\text{would have} + V_3)$

Rule 2 (Imaginary State): In imaginary/hypothetical statements, always use "were" as the auxiliary verb, even if the subject is I, he, she, or it.

Example: If I were the King, I would help the poor.

Desi Mnemonic: "HAD $\rightarrow$ WOULD HAVE"

If the condition uses "had + third form of verb", the result must contain "would have + third form of verb".

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Using "since" with a duration of time, or "for" with a starting date.

Safeguard: Use Since for a fixed point of time (since Monday, since 1995, since 8 AM). Use For for a duration or gap of time (for 3 hours, for 5 days, for 10 years).

Chapter 5: Active & Passive Voice (Direct Shortcuts)

Textbook Definition: Voice describes the relationship between the action of the verb and the subject. In active voice, the subject performs the action. In passive voice, the subject receives the action, marked by a form of auxiliary verb to be $+ V_3$ (past participle).

Simple Explanation: Active voice is "Who did it" (Subject $\rightarrow$ Object). Passive voice is "What happened to it" (Object $\rightarrow$ Subject). During conversion, the tense is never changed, only the verb formula is rearranged.

Examples:

Active: The police arrested the thief.

Passive: The thief was arrested by the police.

Active: Is she preparing dinner?

Passive: Is dinner being prepared by her?

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (The Continuous Verb Rule): When converting active continuous tenses, always insert the word "being" in the passive sentence.


$$\text{is/am/are} + V_4 \rightarrow \text{is/am/are} + \text{being} + V_3$$

$$\text{was/were} + V_4 \rightarrow \text{was/were} + \text{being} + V_3$$

Rule 2 (The Perfect Verb Rule): For perfect tenses, always insert the word "been".


$$\text{has/have} + V_3 \rightarrow \text{has/have} + \text{been} + V_3$$

Rule 3 (Modals): Modals (can, will, must, should) take "be" in passive.


$$\text{can} + V_1 \rightarrow \text{can} + \text{be} + V_3$$

Desi Mnemonic: "OBJECT-FIRST & V3 ALWAYS"

Bring the object to the front seat, push the subject to the back seat with "by", and always use the third form of the verb ($V_3$) no matter what tense it is.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Accidentally changing the tense or time during voice conversion (e.g., turning has into had, or is into was).

Safeguard: Voice changes only the structural shape, never the timeline. If the active sentence is in the present tense, the passive sentence must stay in the present tense!

Chapter 6: Direct & Indirect Speech (Narrational Rules)

Textbook Definition: Direct speech displays the exact spoken words enclosed in quotation marks. Indirect speech conveys the message without quotation marks, shifting tenses, pronouns, and time/place markers to match the reporter's frame of reference.

Simple Explanation: Direct speech is direct quotes. Indirect speech is telling someone else what a person said earlier. Because of this, we must push the tenses backward in time and adjust words like today to that day.

Examples:

Direct: He said, "I want to study now."

Indirect: He said that he wanted to study then.

Direct: She said to me, "Where are you going?"

Indirect: She asked me where I was going. (In questions, do not use "that" and change question format into a normal statement).

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (Tense Back-shifting): If the reporting verb is in the past tense (He said), tenses change as follows:

Simple Present ($V_1/V_5$) $\rightarrow$ Simple Past ($V_2$)

Simple Past ($V_2$) $\rightarrow$ Past Perfect ($\text{had} + V_3$)

Present Continuous $\rightarrow$ Past Continuous

Present Perfect $\rightarrow$ Past Perfect

Will/Can/May $\rightarrow$ Would/Could/Might

Rule 2 (Time & Place shifts):

this $\rightarrow$ that | these $\rightarrow$ those

here $\rightarrow$ there | now $\rightarrow$ then

today $\rightarrow$ that day | tomorrow $\rightarrow$ the next day

Desi Mnemonic: "S-O-N" Connection

Subject $\rightarrow$ Changes according to the 1st Person inside quotation.

Object $\rightarrow$ Changes according to the 2nd Person inside quotation.

No Change $\rightarrow$ For the 3rd Person (he, she, they, it).

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Changing tenses when the reporting verb is in the present tense (He says), or when reporting a universal truth.

Safeguard: If the sentence starts with "He says" or "She will say", or if it is a scientific fact ("The sun rises in the East"), do not change the tense of the quote!

Chapter 7: Prepositions & Fixed Preposition Charts

Textbook Definition: A preposition is a word placed before a noun or pronoun to indicate its relationship (direction, location, time, spatial orientation) to another word in the sentence.

Simple Explanation: Prepositions are locator words. Many English verbs and adjectives are married to one specific "fixed" preposition. Examiners love to test these fixed pairs.

Examples:

Incorrect: He is junior than me by three years.

Correct: He is junior to me by three years. (Words ending in "-ior" like senior, junior, prior, superior take "to").

Incorrect: The students entered into the classroom.

Correct: The students entered the classroom. (Do not use "into" after "enter" when entering physical spaces).

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (Travel vs. Destination): "In" is used for rest/inside location. "Into" shows motion/action toward the inside of something.

Example: He is sitting in the room. vs. He jumped into the well.

Rule 2 (Important Fixed Preposition Pairs):

Abstain / Refrain $\rightarrow$ from

Abide $\rightarrow$ by

Accused $\rightarrow$ of

Congratulate $\rightarrow$ on

Suffer $\rightarrow$ from (but Die $\rightarrow$ of a disease)

Desi Mnemonic: "PRE-POSITION"

"Pre" means before, "Position" means location. It sits before a noun or pronoun to declare its exact coordinate.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Using "of" after "comprise", or "about" after "discuss/describe".

Safeguard: "Comprise" means "consist of", and "discuss" means "talk about". Adding the preposition again makes it redundant.

Wrong: This book comprises of 10 chapters.

Right: This book comprises 10 chapters.

Chapter 8: Articles ($a, an, the$ Placement Traps)

Textbook Definition: Articles are determiners used to clarify whether a noun is specific (Definite Article: The) or non-specific (Indefinite Articles: A/An).

Simple Explanation: Use "a" or "an" when talking about any general thing in the singular form. Use "the" when talking about one specific, unique, or previously mentioned item.

Examples:

Incorrect: He is a European tourist.

Correct: He is a European tourist. (The starting sound of "European" is "Yoo", which is a consonant sound).

Incorrect: Gold is a precious metal.

Correct: Gold is a precious metal. (No article before material nouns unless made specific).

Incorrect: The gold of this ring is pure.

Correct: The gold of this ring is pure. (Specific gold $\rightarrow$ needs "The").

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (The Sound Rule): Use an before nouns beginning with a vowel sound, not just a vowel letter.

An hour (silent 'h'), an honest boy, an MLA, an MP.

A university (starts with 'Y' sound), a one-eyed man (starts with 'W' sound).

Rule 2 (Definite Article "The" Rules): Use "The" for rivers (The Ganga), mountain ranges (The Himalayas), holy books (The Gita), unique structures (The Taj Mahal), and superlative degrees (The tallest boy).

Desi Mnemonic: "HINDI SOUND CHECK"

To choose between a and an, speak the word. If the first sound is a Hindi vowel sound (अ, आ, इ, ई, उ, ऊ, ए, ऐ, ओ, औ), use an. Otherwise, use a.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Writing "The school" or "The hospital" when visiting for primary purposes.

Safeguard: Do not use "the" before school, college, church, hospital, prison, or bed if you are visiting them for their primary purpose (e.g., studying, treatment, sleeping).

Correct: I am going to school. (To study).

Correct: My father came to the school to meet my principal. (Not for study).

PART B: VOCABULARY MASTERY

Chapter 9: Synonyms & Antonyms

Textbook Definition: Synonyms are words with identical or closely related semantic meanings. Antonyms are words expressing diametrically opposite concepts.

Simple Explanation: Synonyms are word twins (similar meaning). Antonyms are opposite words. The best way to learn vocabulary is through root words and visual grouping.

Examples:

Word: Epistle (a letter).

Synonym: Letter, Missive, Communication.

Antonym: Silence, Speech.

Word: Benevolent (kind/helpful).

Synonym: Altruistic, Philanthropic, Generous.

Antonym: Malevolent, Miserly, Cruel.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Fact 1 (Root Word Magic):

Mal- $\rightarrow$ Bad/Evil (Malicious, Malevolent, Malpractice).

Bene- $\rightarrow$ Good/Kind (Benefactor, Benefit, Benign).

Loqu- / Locut- $\rightarrow$ Talk/Speech (Loquacious, Eloquent, Soliloquy).

Fact 2 (Grammatical Balance): Ensure that the synonym/antonym you choose matches the grammatical class of the question word (Noun $\rightarrow$ Noun, Verb $\rightarrow$ Verb).

Desi Mnemonic: "LOQU = LAKHAN"

Think of "Loquacious" as "Lakhan who talks too much". Anyone with "Loqu" in their name talks a lot!

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Choosing an option that is related but represents the wrong part of speech.

Safeguard: Pay attention to the prefix and suffix. A word ending in "-ous" is an adjective, so its synonym will likely end in an adjective form as well.

Chapter 10: One-Word Substitutions

Textbook Definition: The practice of replacing an entire descriptive phrase or sentence with a single, highly specific vocabulary word to make communication concise.

Simple Explanation: Condensing a whole sentence description into one single word. Learning key word endings (suffixes) is the fastest way to master this section.

Examples:

Description: A person who hates mankind.

One-Word: Misanthrope (Root "Mise" = hate + "Anthro" = human).

Description: One who compiles a dictionary.

One-Word: Lexicographer.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (High-Yield Suffixes):

-Cide $\rightarrow$ Act of killing (Uxoricide = killing wife, Regicide = killing king, Fratricide = killing brother).

-Cracy $\rightarrow$ Government system (Plutocracy = by wealthy, Oligarchy = by few, Bureaucracy = by officials).

-Phile $\rightarrow$ Lover of (Bibliophile = lover of books).

Rule 2 (The -ist endings):

Egoist $\rightarrow$ Selfish person focused on personal gains.

Egotist $\rightarrow$ Boastful person who talks constantly about themselves.

Desi Mnemonic: "-CRACY $\rightarrow$ SARKAR"

Whenever you see "-cracy" or "-archy", it means a type of Sarkar (government). Just decode the first half!

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Confusing "Altruist" with "Introvert".

Safeguard: An Altruist is a selfless helper of humanity. An Introvert is simply a quiet person who keeps to themselves. They are completely different concepts!

Chapter 11: Idioms & Phrases

Textbook Definition: An idiom is a figurative phrase whose overall meaning cannot be deduced literally from the individual words composing it.

Simple Explanation: Idioms are colorful expressions that have a hidden meaning. When reading them in exams, never select options that take the words literally.

Examples:

Idiom: A wild goose chase.

Meaning: A useless, hopeless search or journey.

Idiom: A bolt from the blue.

Meaning: A sudden, unexpected shock.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Fact 1 (Action-Based Idioms):

To burn the candle at both ends $\rightarrow$ To work extremely hard or exhaust resources.

To hit the nail on the head $\rightarrow$ To do or say the exact right thing.

Fact 2: SSC repeatedly asks idioms based on colors (red letter day = important day, green horn = inexperienced person).

Desi Mnemonic: "SPILL THE BEANS"

Imagine opening a bag of secret beans and accidentally dropping them everywhere. The secret is out! It means revealing a secret.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Selecting options that talk about physical items mentioned in the idiom.

Safeguard: If the idiom is "To smell a rat", the correct answer option will never contain the words "animal", "mouse", or "trap". It will always be about "suspecting a trick".

Chapter 12: Spelling Correction Hacks

Textbook Definition: The study of standard orthography. Spelling correction tests identify common phonetical mistakes and silent letter traps.

Simple Explanation: Easy-to-remember rules to help you avoid writing words with incorrect letters, especially words with double vowels or silent sounds.

Examples:

Incorrect: Gage, Millenium, Occasion.

Correct: Gauge, Millennium (Double L, Double N), Occasion (Double C, Single S).

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (The classic 'ie' vs 'ei' rule): Use i before e, except after the letter c (when sounding like "ee").

After 'C': Receive, Ceiling, Deceive.

After other letters: Believe, Relief, Chief.

Rule 2 (Double Letter Trap): Words with multiple double letters are examiners' favorites:

Committee $\rightarrow$ 2 'm', 2 't', 2 'e'.

Embarrass $\rightarrow$ 2 'r', 2 's'.

Harass $\rightarrow$ 1 'r', 2 's' (Notice the difference!).

Desi Mnemonic: "LIEU-TENANT"

How to write the spelling of Lieutenant: Break it into French/English words: Lie + u + Tenant (झूठ + तुम + किराएदार).

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Confusing the spellings of "Independent" and "Dependant".

Safeguard: Remember that Independent ends with "-ent" (all 'e's!), while Dependant can end with "-ant" when used as a noun.

PART C: COMPREHENSION & STRUCTURE

Chapter 13: Spotting the Error (Mixed Practice Rules)

Textbook Definition: Identifying syntactical, grammatical, or punctuation errors across segmented sections of a sentence.

Simple Explanation: Finding the exact grammatical mistake in a sentence divided into parts. It requires checking the subject, tenses, and modifiers in a structured order.

Examples:

Sentence: Scarcely had I arrived (A) / than it started (B) / raining heavily (C) / No Error (D).

Error: Part B is wrong. "Scarcely" must always be followed by when, never "than".

Sentence: He is one of those men (A) / who does not (B) / tell lies (C).

Error: Part B is wrong. "who" refers to the plural "men", so the verb must be plural: "do not".

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (Correlative Conjunction Pairs):

No sooner $\rightarrow$ paired with than (not then or when)

Hardly / Scarcely $\rightarrow$ paired with when

Lest $\rightarrow$ paired with should (never takes not)

Rule 2 (Noun-Adjective errors): Double comparatives are wrong (e.g., "more taller" is incorrect; use "taller" or "much taller").

Desi Mnemonic: "S-P-V FIRST"

First check the Subject, then the Pronoun, then the Verb tense. This simple check identifies more than $70\%$ of error spotting questions!

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Forgetting that "Lest" has a negative meaning built-in.

Safeguard: Never write "lest... should not". Always use "lest... should" + positive action.

Wrong: Run fast lest you should not lose the race.

Right: Run fast lest you should lose the race.

Chapter 14: Sentence Improvement Tactics

Textbook Definition: Evaluating a designated sentence segment to determine if it is grammatically suboptimal, and replacing it with the most syntactically precise alternative.

Simple Explanation: Choosing the best phrase option to replace a highlighted part of a sentence. If the sentence has no errors, the option "No Improvement" is your answer.

Examples:

Sentence: The climate of Patna is better than Ranchi.

Improvement: The climate of Patna is better than that of Ranchi. (We must compare the climate of Patna with the climate of Ranchi, not with the physical city of Ranchi itself).

Sentence: If I will go to Delhi, I will bring a gift.

Improvement: If I go to Delhi, I will bring a gift.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (Parallel Structure): Items in a series or comparison must have the same structural format.

Incorrect: He likes swimming and to run.

Correct: He likes swimming and running.

Rule 2 (Redundancy Elimination): Do not use words that repeat meanings.

Wrong: Please repeat back the answer.

Right: Please repeat the answer.

Desi Mnemonic: "THAT OF / THOSE OF"

Use "that of" for comparing singular nouns (climate, cost) and "those of" for comparing plural nouns (streets, people).

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Automatically assuming every sentence must be improved.

Safeguard: "No Improvement" is the correct choice in around $15\%$ of all questions. If the sentence doesn't break any grammar rules, trust your core rules and select "No Improvement".

Chapter 15: Cloze Test Elimination Tricks

Textbook Definition: A semantic evaluation exercise where multiple words are deleted from a continuous passage, requiring the reader to restore the words using context, tone, grammar, and collocations.

Simple Explanation: A story with gaps where you choose words that fit. To ace this, look at the general story mood (tone) and look at the grammar clues around each blank space.

Examples:

Passage Segment: "The doctor advised him to ______ smoking immediately to protect his failing lungs."

Options: quit / give up / drop / escape

Best Choice: give up (collocates perfectly with smoking as a habit).

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Trick 1 (The Tone Filter): Read the whole passage in 30 seconds before filling in blanks. If the story has a negative tone, eliminate positive words immediately.

Trick 2 (Grammar clues): Look at the words directly before and after the blank. If the word after the blank is "to", a verb that takes "to" (like object, look forward) must be selected.

Desi Mnemonic: "WORD PARTNERSHIPS" (Collocations)

Words have natural partners. We "make a mistake" (not "do a mistake"), we "take an exam" (not "give an exam" in correct English), and we "pay attention". Look for these natural word partnerships!

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Answering blanks one by one without reading the surrounding sentences.

Safeguard: Sometimes the perfect word for Blank 2 is literally written in the sentence of Blank 4 as a hint! Read ahead.

Chapter 16: Para Jumbles (Rearrangement Connectives)

Textbook Definition: Reordering a set of disjointed sentences into a logically coherent, cohesive paragraph.

Simple Explanation: Sorting a scrambled set of 4 sentences (usually labeled P, Q, R, S) into the correct logical order. Use pronoun connections and starting clues to match them up fast.

Examples:

P: He is widely known as the father of the Nation.

Q: Mahatma Gandhi was born on 2nd October.

R: This day is celebrated as Gandhi Jayanti.

Correct Order: Q $\rightarrow$ P $\rightarrow$ R (We introduce "Mahatma Gandhi" first, use the pronoun "He" second, and describe "this day" third).

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (The Opening Clue): The first sentence of a paragraph is almost always an independent statement introducing a concept, name, or event. It will rarely start with connectors like But, However, Therefore, Thus, He, It.

Rule 2 (Mandatory Pairs): Find two sentences that must go together.

Example: Sentence A introduces a problem $\rightarrow$ Sentence B offers a solution. This is a mandatory pair.

Desi Mnemonic: "NOUN $\rightarrow$ PRONOUN"

Noun always comes before its pronoun substitute. Find the sentence with the actual name, and place it before the sentence with He, She, It, or They.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Trying to read and arrange all sentences in your head without looking at the option choices.

Safeguard: Use the options as a elimination map. If options only start with Q or R, don't waste time trying to make P the opening sentence!

SUBJECT 2: QUANTITATIVE APTITUDE

PART A: ARITHMETIC

Chapter 17: Number System & Simplification

Textbook Definition: The structured representation of numbers. Simplification involves calculating numerical expressions using the mathematical order of operations known as BODMAS.

Simple Explanation: The study of how numbers behave (primes, factors, divisibility) and how to solve equations quickly using order rules.

Examples:

Solve: 

$$40 \div 5 \text{ of } 2 + 10 - (3 \times 2)$$

Step-by-step Solution:

Brackets first: $(3 \times 2) = 6$. Expression becomes $40 \div 5 \text{ of } 2 + 10 - 6$.

"Of" second: $5 \text{ of } 2 = 5 \times 2 = 10$. Expression becomes $40 \div 10 + 10 - 6$.

Division third: $40 \div 10 = 4$. Expression becomes $4+10-6$.

Addition/Subtraction: $14 - 6 = 8$.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Divisibility Rules:

3 & 9: Sum of all digits must be divisible by 3 or 9.

11: The difference between the sum of digits at odd places and even places must be 0 or a multiple of 11.

Unit Digit Cyclicity: The unit digits of numbers repeat every $4$ powers. Divide the power by $4$, and use the remainder as the new power to find the unit digit quickly.

Desi Mnemonic: "BODMAS"

Brackets $\rightarrow$ Of $\rightarrow$ Division $\rightarrow$ Multiplication $\rightarrow$ Addition $\rightarrow$ Subtraction. Always solve inside-out!

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Solving division before "Of" because division is written first in common speech.

Safeguard: "Of" is mathematically a multiplication that holds higher priority than division. Always calculate the "Of" part first!

Chapter 18: Decimals and Fractions

Textbook Definition: A fraction represents a part of a whole, defined as a numerator over a denominator. Recurring decimals represent fractions with repeating decimal values.

Simple Explanation: Simple tricks to compare which fraction is bigger and how to convert repeating decimals (like $0.333...$) into exact fractions.

Examples:

Question: Convert $0.3\bar{7}$ (7 repeats) into a fraction.

Solution: 

$$0.3\bar{7} = \frac{37 - 3}{90} = \frac{34}{90} = \frac{17}{45}$$

Question: Which fraction is larger: $\frac{5}{8}$ or $\frac{7}{11}$?

Solution: Cross-multiply: $5 \times 11 = 55$ and $8 \times 7 = 56$. Since $56 > 55$, then $\frac{7}{11} > \frac{5}{8}$.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Rule 1 (Bar to Fraction Conversion):

For pure recurring decimals: Put a $9$ in the denominator for every digit under the bar.


$$0.\bar{53} = \frac{53}{99}$$

For mixed recurring decimals: Numerator = (Full number - non-repeating part). Denominator = (A '9' for each repeating digit followed by a '0' for each non-repeating digit).

Desi Mnemonic: "9 FOR BAR, 0 FOR BLANK"

In the bottom of the fraction: Write 9 for digits with a bar overhead, and write 0 for digits after the decimal that have no bar.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Forgetting to subtract the non-repeating digits from the top number.

Safeguard: In $0.2\bar{4}$, the numerator is NOT 24. It is $24 - 2 = 22$. Thus, the fraction is $\frac{22}{90} = \frac{11}{45}$.

Chapter 19: Percentage, Profit, Loss & Discount

Textbook Definition: Percentage is a relative value indicating hundredth parts of any quantity. Profit, Loss, and Discount represent commercial relationships between Cost Price (CP), Selling Price (SP), and Marked Price (MP).

Simple Explanation: How to calculate price hikes, shopkeeper discounts, and net profits using simple formula shortcuts.

Examples:

Question: If A's income is $25\%$ more than B's, how much % is B's income less than A's?

Solution: 

$$\text{Less \%} = \frac{r}{100 + r} \times 100 = \frac{25}{125} \times 100 = 20\%$$

Question: A shopkeeper offers successive discounts of $20\%$ and $10\%$. Find the single equivalent discount.

Solution: Use successive formula:


$$\text{Discount} = a + b - \frac{ab}{100} = 20 + 10 - \frac{20 \times 10}{100} = 30 - 2 = 28\%$$

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Formula 1 (Successive Change):


$$\text{Net Change \%} = a + b + \frac{ab}{100}$$

 (Use positive for profit/increase, negative for loss/discount).

Formula 2 (Marked Price to Cost Price Ratio):


$$\frac{\text{MP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{100 + \text{Profit \%}}{100 - \text{Discount \%}}$$

Desi Mnemonic: "MP-D-SP-P-CP"

Discount is always subtracted from the Marked Price (MP) to find the selling price (SP). Profit/Loss is always calculated on the Cost Price (CP) to find the selling price (SP).

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Calculating the discount percentage directly on the cost price (CP).

Safeguard: Always remember that discount is strictly calculated on the Marked Price (MP) printed on the label!

Chapter 20: Ratio, Proportion & Partnership

Textbook Definition: Ratio is a quantitative comparison of two numbers. Partnership is a business structure where two or more entities share profits based on investment capital and duration.

Simple Explanation: How to merge split ratios and how to calculate profits in a shared business based on money spent and time elapsed.

Examples:

Question: If $A:B = 2:3$ and $B:C = 4:5$, find $A:B:C$.

Solution (Balancing Method): Multiply the first ratio by 4 and the second by 3 to make the common term "B" equal ($12$):


$$A:B = 8:12, \quad B:C = 12:15 \quad \rightarrow \quad A:B:C = 8:12:15$$

Question: Ram invests ₹5000 for 10 months and Shyam invests ₹4000 for 5 months. Split a profit of ₹28,000.

Solution: Profit ratio:


$$\text{Ram} : \text{Shyam} = (5000 \times 10) : (4000 \times 5) = 50000 : 20000 = 5:2$$

$$\text{Ram's share} = \frac{5}{7} \times 28000 = ₹20,000$$

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Partnership Formula:


$$\text{Profit Ratio} = (I_1 \times T_1) : (I_2 \times T_2)$$

Proportion Types:

Third Proportion of $a$ and $b$ $\rightarrow \frac{b^2}{a}$

Fourth Proportion of $a, b, c$ $\rightarrow \frac{bc}{a}$

Desi Mnemonic: "PIT FORMULA"

Profit = Investment $\times$ Time. It is the golden rule of any joint business question.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Splitting the profit purely based on investment money, ignoring that one partner stayed in the business longer.

Safeguard: Always multiply the money by the months before writing down the profit-sharing ratio!

Chapter 21: Averages, Mixtures & Alligations

Textbook Definition: Average is the central value of a set of data. Alligation is a mathematical method used to determine the ratio in which ingredients at different prices must be mixed to obtain a mixture of a desired average cost.

Simple Explanation: Finding the middle value, and using the diagonal subtraction trick (Alligation) to find mixing ratios for liquids or groups.

Examples:

Question: The average age of 10 students is 15 years. When a teacher joins, the average increases by 1 year. Find the teacher's age.

Solution: 

$$\text{New Total Age (11 people)} = 11 \times 16 = 176$$

$$\text{Old Total Age (10 people)} = 10 \times 15 = 150$$

$$\text{Teacher's Age} = 176 - 150 = 26 \text{ years}$$

Question: Mix tea worth ₹60/kg with tea worth ₹80/kg to get a blend worth ₹72/kg. Find the ratio.

Solution: Apply Alligation:

Left difference: $|80 - 72| = 8$

Right difference: $|60 - 72| = 12$

Ratio is $8 : 12 = 2 : 3$.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Average Speed Formula (Equal Distances):


$$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{2xy}{x+y}$$

Alligation Cross Map:


$$\frac{\text{Quantity of Cheaper}}{\text{Quantity of Dearer}} = \frac{\text{Dearer Price} - \text{Mean Price}}{\text{Mean Price} - \text{Cheaper Price}}$$

Desi Mnemonic: "CHALO DIAGONAL"

In Alligation: High price on top-left, Low price on top-right, Target price in the middle. Subtract diagonally to find the bottom ratio.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Using $\frac{x+y}{2}$ for average speed when a vehicle travels two halves of a journey at different speeds.

Safeguard: This simple average is only correct if the travel times are equal. If the distances are equal, you must use the harmonic mean formula: $\frac{2xy}{x+y}$.

Chapter 22: Simple & Compound Interest (The 3-Year Ratio Trick)

Textbook Definition: Simple Interest (SI) is interest accumulated on the principal amount alone. Compound Interest (CI) is interest reinvested, meaning it is calculated on the principal plus any accumulated interest from previous periods.

Simple Explanation: Simple interest pays the same fee every year. Compound interest is "interest on interest". Instead of long formulas with big powers, use our simple ratio multipliers to solve interest questions.

Examples:

Question: Find CI on ₹20,000 at $10\%$ rate for 3 years.

Solution (Using the 3-Year Ratio Trick): The 3-year multiplier is $3:3:1$.

Calculate $10\%$ of principal: $10\% \text{ of } 20,000 = 2000$.

Calculate $10\%$ of previous interest: $10\% \text{ of } 2000 = 200$.

Calculate $10\%$ of previous interest: $10\% \text{ of } 200 = 20$.

Multiply and add: $(2000 \times 3) + (200 \times 3) + (20 \times 1) = 6000 + 600 + 20 = ₹6620$.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

SI Formula:


$$\text{SI} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$$

Difference between CI and SI Shortcuts:

For 2 Years:


$$D_2 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$$

For 3 Years:


$$D_3 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \times \left(\frac{300 + R}{100}\right)$$

Desi Mnemonic: "INTEREST RATIO SET"

2 Years $\rightarrow$ Multiply by $2:1$

3 Years $\rightarrow$ Multiply by $3:3:1$

4 Years $\rightarrow$ Multiply by $4:6:4:1$

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Forgetting to change the rate and time when interest is calculated "half-yearly".

Safeguard: Divide the Rate by 2 ($R \rightarrow R/2$) and multiply the Time years by 2 ($T \rightarrow 2T$) before starting any calculations!

Chapter 23: Time & Work & Pipes & Cisterns

Textbook Definition: The mathematical relationship between work completed, time allocated, and the rate of efficiency of workers. In fluid dynamics (pipes), inlet pipes act as positive work, while outlet drain pipes represent negative work.

Simple Explanation: How long it takes to complete a task. Use the LCM method to find "Total Work units" instead of calculating with messy fractions.

Examples:

Question: Pipe A can fill a tank in 12 hours. Pipe B can empty the full tank in 15 hours. If both open, how long to fill?

Solution (LCM Method): Let Total Work = LCM(12, 15) = 60 units.

Filling efficiency of A = $60 / 12 = +5$ units/hour.

Emptying efficiency of B = $60 / 15 = -4$ units/hour.

Combined efficiency = $+5 - 4 = +1$ unit/hour.

Time to fill = $60 / 1 = 60$ hours.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Formula 1 (The MDH Rule):


$$\frac{M_1 \times D_1 \times H_1}{W_1} = \frac{M_2 \times D_2 \times H_2}{W_2}$$

 (Where M = Men, D = Days, H = Hours, W = Work done or Wages earned).

Property 2: Efficiency is inversely related to time taken:


$$\frac{\text{Efficiency of A}}{\text{Efficiency of B}} = \frac{\text{Time taken by B}}{\text{Time taken by A}}$$

Desi Mnemonic: "ASLI MASALE SACH SACH"

M-D-H on top, Work on the bottom. Keep this balanced equation on your paper to solve any grouping question.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Adding the individual days directly (e.g., saying A and B together will take $10+15=25$ days).

Safeguard: Never add days directly! Convert days to efficiencies first, add the efficiencies, and then divide total work by the combined efficiency.

Chapter 24: Time, Speed & Distance (Trains, Boats & Streams)

Textbook Definition: Speed is the rate at which an object covers distance. Relative speed represents the speed of an object with respect to another moving object, depending on direction.

Simple Explanation: Tracking travel speed. For trains, remember that passing a platform means crossing the train's own length plus the platform's length. For boats, going down-river makes you faster, going up-river makes you slower.

Examples:

Question: Convert 72 km/h into meters per second (m/s).

Solution: 

$$72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 4 \times 5 = 20 \text{ m/s}$$

Question: A train 150m long crosses a platform 250m long in 20 seconds. Find its speed in km/h.

Solution: 

$$\text{Total Distance} = 150 + 250 = 400 \text{ meters}$$

$$\text{Speed} = \frac{400 \text{ m}}{20 \text{ s}} = 20 \text{ m/s}$$

$$\text{Speed in km/h} = 20 \times \frac{18}{5} = 72 \text{ km/h}$$

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Relative Speed Rule:

Objects moving in opposite directions $\rightarrow$ Add speeds ($S_1 + S_2$).

Objects moving in the same direction $\rightarrow$ Subtract speeds ($S_1 - S_2$).

Boat Speeds:

Downstream speed (with current) $D = u + v$

Upstream speed (against current) $U = u - v$

Boat speed in still water $u = \frac{D+U}{2}$, Stream speed $v = \frac{D-U}{2}$

Desi Mnemonic: "DOWN STREAM IS FAST, UP IS ANGRY"

Going downstream is fast because you add water speed ($u+v$). Going upstream is tough and slow because you subtract water speed ($u-v$).

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Forgetting to include the length of the platform or another train when calculating train crossing times.

Safeguard: A train crossing a pole or a standing man covers only its own length ($L$). A train crossing a platform, bridge, or tunnel covers its own length plus the obstacle's length ($L_1 + L_2$).

PART B: ADVANCED MATHEMATICS

Chapter 25: Algebra (Basic Identities)

Textbook Definition: The mathematical study of variables, symbols, and structural equations. Standard algebraic identities are used to factorize and simplify higher-degree equations.

Simple Explanation: Using letters in formulas to solve equations. Memorizing a few key power shortcuts helps you find answers without working out large multiplications.

Examples:

Question: If $x + \frac{1}{x} = 4$, find $x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2}$ and $x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3}$.

Solution (Using Shortcuts):

For $x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2}$:


$$k^2 - 2 = 4^2 - 2 = 16 - 2 = 14$$

For $x^3 + \frac{1}{x^3}$:


$$k^3 - 3k = 4^3 - 3(4) = 64 - 12 = 52$$

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Standard Algebra Identities:


$$(a+b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + 2ab$$

$$(a-b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab$$

$$(a^2 - b^2) = (a-b)(a+b)$$

The Zero Sum Trick:


$$\text{If } a + b + c = 0 \rightarrow a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 3abc$$

Desi Mnemonic: "ALGEBRA POWER TRICK"

If $x + \frac{1}{x} = k$:

Squared answer is $k^2 - 2$

Cubed answer is $k^3 - 3k$

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Forgetting to check if $a+b+c=0$ in complex-looking cubic fraction questions.

Safeguard: Look at the values inside the brackets. If adding them up results in 0, replace the entire numerator with $3abc$ immediately to solve it in seconds.

Chapter 26: Geometry (Triangles, Circles & Tangents)

Textbook Definition: The study of properties and spatial relations of points, lines, angles, triangles, and circular theorems (including tangent equations).

Simple Explanation: Solving properties of shapes. Triangle angles, circle theorems, and the formulas for tangent lines that touch the outside of a circle.

Examples:

Question: Two circles of radii 9cm and 4cm have centers that are 13cm apart. Find the length of their Direct Common Tangent (DCT).

Solution: 

$$\text{DCT Length} = \sqrt{d^2 - (r_1 - r_2)^2} = \sqrt{13^2 - (9 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{169 - 25} = \sqrt{144} = 12\text{cm}$$

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Triangle Properties:

The sum of all three angles is $180^\circ$.

Incenter Angle: $\angle\text{BIC} = 90^\circ + \frac{\angle A}{2}$

Circumcenter Angle: $\angle\text{BOC} = 2\angle A$

Tangent Formula Shortcuts:

$$\text{Direct Common Tangent (DCT)} = \sqrt{d^2 - (r_1 - r_2)^2}$$

$$\text{Transverse Common Tangent (TCT)} = \sqrt{d^2 - (r_1 + r_2)^2}$$

 (Where $d$ is the distance between circle centers).

Desi Mnemonic: "RADIUS MEET TANGENT AT 90"

A circle's radius and a tangent line touching the edge always form a sharp $90^\circ$ right angle at the point of contact. Use Pythagoras' theorem from there!

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Using the DCT formula when the question asks for the Transverse Common Tangent (TCT).

Safeguard: Notice the sign inside the radius brackets. DCT uses subtraction of radii ($(r_1 - r_2)^2$), while TCT uses addition of radii ($(r_1 + r_2)^2$).

Chapter 27: Mensuration (2D & 3D Formulas)

Textbook Definition: The branch of geometry dealing with the measurement of length, area, perimeter, and volume of 2D shapes and 3D solids.

Simple Explanation: Measuring shapes. Calculating flat surface areas (2D) or storage volume capacities of 3D objects like cones, spheres, and cylinders.

Examples:

Question: Find the total surface area (TSA) of a solid hemisphere of radius 7cm.

Solution: 

$$\text{TSA of Hemisphere} = 3\pi r^2 = 3 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 = 3 \times 22 \times 7 = 462 \text{ cm}^2$$

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

2D Cheat Sheet:

Area of Equilateral Triangle $\rightarrow \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2$

Circle Area $\rightarrow \pi r^2$, Circumference $\rightarrow 2\pi r$

3D Cheat Sheet:

Sphere Volume $\rightarrow \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$

Hemisphere Curved Surface Area (CSA) $\rightarrow 2\pi r^2$, Total Surface Area (TSA) $\rightarrow 3\pi r^2$

Cone Volume $\rightarrow \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$, Slant height $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}$

Desi Mnemonic: "DIVISIBLE BY 11"

Any formula that has $\pi$ in it (like circle areas, cylinder volumes, etc.) contains $\frac{22}{7}$ ($2 \times 11$). Therefore, the correct answer option must be divisible by 11. Test the options for divisibility by 11 to save time!

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Using $2\pi r^2$ for the Total Surface Area of a hemisphere.

Safeguard: $2\pi r^2$ is only the curved bowl area (CSA). A solid hemisphere has a flat circular top, so its Total Surface Area (TSA) is $3\pi r^2$.

Chapter 28: Trigonometry (Heights & Distances)

Textbook Definition: The branch of mathematics dealing with relations of sides and angles of right-angled triangles, and calculations of height and distance based on trigonometric ratios.

Simple Explanation: Measuring heights using angles of sight. You can find the height of a building or a tree using simple triangle ratios without climbing it.

Examples:

Question: A kite is flying with a thread of length 100m, making an angle of $30^\circ$ with the ground. Find the height of the kite.

Solution: 

$$\sin(30^\circ) = \frac{\text{Height}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} \rightarrow \frac{1}{2} = \frac{H}{100} \rightarrow H = 50 \text{ meters}$$

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Basic Ratios:


$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{Perpendicular}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}, \quad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{Base}}{\text{Hypotenuse}}, \quad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{Perpendicular}}{\text{Base}}$$

The Height & Distance Triplet Ratio Shortcuts:

For a $30^\circ-60^\circ-90^\circ$ triangle, the side lengths are always in the ratio $1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$.

For a $45^\circ-45^\circ-90^\circ$ triangle, the side lengths are always in the ratio $1 : 1 : \sqrt{2}$.

Desi Mnemonic: "PANDIT BADRI PRASAD"

Remember the ratio line: Pandit Badri Prasad / Hari Hari Bol.

$$\sin = \frac{\text{P}}{\text{H}}, \quad \cos = \frac{\text{B}}{\text{H}}, \quad \tan = \frac{\text{P}}{\text{B}}$$

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Getting confused about when to use $\sin$ versus $\tan$.

Safeguard: If the question involves the ground shadow and vertical wall, use $\tan$ (Base and Perpendicular). If it involves a ladder, rope, or kite string, use $\sin$ (Hypotenuse and Perpendicular).

Chapter 29: Statistical Charts

Textbook Definition: Quantitative analysis of numerical data represented in visual charts (Pie Charts, Histograms, Bar Graphs). Central tendencies are measured using Mean, Median, and Mode.

Simple Explanation: Reading charts to find percentages or averages, and calculating central middle values.

Examples:

Question: Find the Median of the set: $12, 15, 11, 19, 13$.

Solution: 1. Arrange in ascending order: $11, 12, 13, 15, 19$.
2. The middle number is the 3rd one. Median = 13.

Question: A student spends $10\%$ of his budget on books. Represent this in degrees on a circular Pie Chart.

Solution: A full circle is $360^\circ$.


$$\text{Angle} = 10\% \text{ of } 360^\circ = 36^\circ$$

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

The Empirical Formula (Highly Tested):


$$\text{Mode} = 3 \times \text{Median} - 2 \times \text{Mean}$$

Average Value Formulas:

Mean $\rightarrow$ Sum of values / total count.

Median $\rightarrow$ Middle value when sorted.

Mode $\rightarrow$ Most frequent number in the set.

Desi Mnemonic: "MODE IS POPULAR"

Mode is simply the most popular, repeating value in your number group.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Calculating the Median directly from the jumbled set as written in the question.

Safeguard: You must sort the numbers from smallest to largest before picking the middle number for your Median!

Chapter 33: Syllogisms & Venn Diagrams

Textbook Definition: Deductive reasoning to test the validity of conclusions based on given logical premises, solved using overlapping geometric diagrams.

Simple Explanation: Checking if statements like "All pens are papers" make other conclusions true. Draw overlapping circles to represent the statements and find the truth.

Examples:

Statements: All boys are singers. Some singers are smart.

Conclusions: 1. Some boys are smart. (Not necessarily true - no guaranteed overlap).
2. Some singers are boys. (True - the "singers" circle overlaps with the internal "boys" circle).

Answer: Only conclusion 2 follows.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Circle Relationships:

All A are B: Circle A is drawn completely inside circle B.

Some A are B: Circle A overlaps partially with circle B.

No A is B: Circle A and circle B are drawn as separate, unconnected shapes.

Desi Mnemonic: "MINIMUM OVERLAP RULE"

When drawing Venn diagrams, only overlap circles where the statements explicitly force you to. If a conclusion is false in even one possible diagram, it is incorrect.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Applying real-world facts or biology to logic puzzles.

Safeguard: If a statement says "All dogs are cats", accept it as 100% true on your paper. Forget real-world animal facts and trust only your circles!

Chapter 35: Embedded Figures & Mirror Images

Textbook Definition: Spatial visualization tasks. Embedded figure tests require isolating a target shape within a complex pattern. Mirror/water image tests require drawing left-to-right lateral inversions or top-to-bottom vertical inversions.

Simple Explanation: Finding a hidden pattern inside a bigger design, or guessing what a word looks like when reflected in a mirror or water.

Water Image (Horizontal mirror on bottom): Top and Bottom swap. Left and Right stay the same.

The parts of the image closest to the mirror line must stay closest to the mirror line in your reflection drawing.

Trap: Accidentally turning the image upside down when a standard vertical mirror image is requested.

Chapter 37: Indian Polity (Fundamental Rights & Articles)

Textbook Definition: The structural framework of the Constitution of India, detailing constitutional articles, schedules, fundamental liberties, and the operations of state institutions.

Simple Explanation: The rules of Indian law, your basic human rights, and the key articles from our constitution that you must memorize.

Examples:

Question: Which article deals with the "Abolition of Untouchability"?

Solution: Article 17 (under the Fundamental Right to Equality).

Question: How many fundamental duties are in the Constitution, and how were they added?

Solution: 11 Fundamental Duties (Article 51A). They were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976 on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12-35): Borrowed from the USA.

Article 14: Equality before law.

Article 19: Freedom of speech.

Article 21: Right to life and personal liberty.

Article 32: Right to Constitutional Remedies (Dr. Ambedkar called it the "Heart and Soul" of the constitution).

Key Constitutional Bodies:

Article 280: Finance Commission.

Article 324: Election Commission.

Desi Mnemonic: "TEARS OF OLD PM"

The 12 Schedules of the Indian Constitution:

Territories, Emoluments, Affirmations, Rajya Sabha, Scheduled Areas, Other Scheduled Areas, Federal Structure, Official Languages, Land Reforms, Defection (Anti-Defection Law), Panchayats, Municipalities.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Thinking that the Right to Property is still a Fundamental Right.

Safeguard: The Right to Property was removed from the Fundamental Rights list by the 44th Amendment in 1978. It is now only a legal right under Article 300A.

Chapter 39: Economy (National Income & Budget)

Textbook Definition: The structural dynamics of macroeconomics, focusing on National Income indices (GDP, GNP, NNP), budgetary fiscal policies, inflation types, and banking regulations set by the RBI.

Simple Explanation: How money works on a national level. Concepts of Gross Domestic Product (GDP), inflation, and the different banking rates set by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).

Examples:

Question: What is the mathematical difference between GDP (Gross Domestic Product) and GNP (Gross National Product)?

Solution: 

$$\text{GNP} = \text{GDP} + \text{Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)}$$

Question: What does "Stagflation" mean?

Solution: A dangerous economic condition where inflation is high, but economic growth is slow/stagnant, causing high unemployment.

Key Core Concepts & Facts:

National Income Formulas:

$$\text{Net Domestic Product (NDP)} = \text{GDP} - \text{Depreciation}$$

 (Wear and tear cost).

RBI Monetary Rates:

Repo Rate: Interest rate at which RBI lends money to commercial banks.

Reverse Repo Rate: Interest rate banks earn when they park funds with RBI.

CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio): The percentage of deposits banks must keep as cash with RBI.

Desi Mnemonic: "NET MEANS DEPRECIATION GONE"

In National Income economics: Whenever you want to find a "Net" value (like NDP or NNP) from a "Gross" value (like GDP or GNP), always subtract Depreciation.

Memory Box "Trap":

Trap: Thinking that paper currency notes are printed entirely by the RBI.

Safeguard: All currency notes are printed by RBI except the One Rupee Note/Coin, which is signed and issued directly by the Finance Secretary of the Government of India!
